EXPERIENCE / OTTOBOCK

Ottobock

Data Science Intern · February 2026 – August 2026

Ottobock develops medical technologies, including prosthetics and orthotics, that support mobility and independence. I worked in a research environment, building reusable Python workflows to prepare complex 3D and clinical data, implement modelling experiments, and evaluate predictions.

90+ datasetsApproximately 40,000 sampled 3D points each

LOOCVAcross 90+ cases

Data collection and metadata extraction

The project involved both 3D files and supporting metadata stored in PDFs. I built workflows using Python and pdfplumber to extract that information, organise it into structured tables, and translate Danish text into English so it could be used consistently alongside the 3D data.

Data inspection and cleaning

Before modelling, I worked through the data to understand its structure and prepare it for consistent processing.

  • Cleaned and integrated extracted metadata using Python, pandas, and NumPy.
  • Inspected 3D meshes and point samples to assess their suitability for preprocessing.
  • Developed standardisation and quality-validation workflows across 90+ datasets, using approximately 40,000 sampled 3D points per case.

3D preprocessing and registration

I developed reusable workflows for alignment, mesh cutting, Laplacian smoothing, mirroring, and point registration using Python and Trimesh. Comparing 3+ registration methods and 4+ processing configurations helped me investigate how preparation choices affected the modelling workflow.

Predictive modelling

I adapted an existing modelling implementation to the project’s requirements, using TensorFlow and Principal Component Analysis (PCA) to support experiments with high-dimensional 3D data. I developed the surrounding extraction, preprocessing, and evaluation workflows myself, making the steps reusable for further experiments.

Model testing and evaluation

I automated leave-one-out cross-validation (LOOCV) across 90+ cases. Each round held one case back for testing while the model learned from the remaining cases, allowing me to assess predictions on data excluded from that round’s training.

I examined prediction quality using statistical metrics and data visualisations, investigating where results worked well or fell short and discussing the findings with engineering stakeholders.

Unseen cases and collaboration

I also analysed predictions across 4+ additional unseen datasets, identified performance gaps, and translated the findings into recommendations for further investigation. Working with colleagues from different backgrounds helped me connect the numerical results with practical considerations.

What I enjoyed the most?

What stayed with me was seeing how AI and machine learning could contribute to work that improves people’s lives. I enjoyed getting to understand a new medical problem, learning from colleagues with different backgrounds, and hearing how they approached it. Building reusable workflows that could support further research made my contribution feel particularly meaningful.

Research confidentiality

This work formed part of a research project. Detailed methods, model architecture, and sensitive project materials cannot be shared publicly.
